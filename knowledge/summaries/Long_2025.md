# Long et al. (2025) - Basic Considerations for Consistency Evaluation Based on ICH E17

## Citation
Long M, Wu H, Liu X, Li P, Lin F, Zhou Z, Xia X, Zhu C, Ji C, Zhang W, Zhang K, Yu B, Wang Y, Zhang H, Jia F, Hou Y (2025) "Basic Considerations for the Consistency Evaluation Based on ICH E17 Guideline" *Therapeutic Innovation & Regulatory Science* 59:328-336
DOI: [10.1007/s43441-024-00717-z](https://doi.org/10.1007/s43441-024-00717-z)

## Summary
This paper provides practical guidance for implementing consistency evaluation in multi-regional clinical trials (MRCTs) based on the ICH E17 guideline. Written from the Chinese regulatory (NMPA/CDE) perspective, it elaborates on study design considerations, interpretation of results, and special scenarios. The authors propose a holistic approach to consistency evaluation covering epidemiology, clinical pharmacology, intrinsic/extrinsic factors, pooling strategy, and sample size allocation. They also discuss statistical models including fixed-effect, random-effect, and Bayesian hierarchical approaches.

## Key Contributions
- Practical guidance for ICH E17 consistency evaluation implementation
- Holistic approach: recommends evaluating consistency based on several candidate criteria rather than a fixed single criterion (per ICH E17)
- Distinguishes **internal consistency** (within study: across endpoints, subgroups) from **external consistency** (across studies, historical data)
- Discusses special considerations: non-inferiority, multiple primary endpoints, interim analyses, single arm trials, adaptive design, rare disease
- Proposes three-level Bayesian hierarchical model structure (studies → subgroups → patients)
- Chinese (NMPA/CDE) and FDA perspectives on regional consistency

## Study Design Considerations

### Epidemiology & Current Treatment
- Disease epidemiological characteristics are crucial for MRCT design
- Regional differences in incidence/prevalence, risk factors, histological conditions warrant investigation
- Disease background and treatment landscape may vary by region

### Clinical Pharmacology
- PK, pharmacodynamics (PD), dose-exposure-response assessment
- ADME characteristics, genetic data (genotypes of drug metabolizing enzymes)
- Early phase MRCTs should understand ethnicity impact on PK and PK-PD
- Bridging studies (ICH E5 context) for dose optimization

### Intrinsic and Extrinsic Factors, Pooling Strategy
- Pooling strategies facilitate consistency evaluation across regions
- ICH E17 recommends balanced approach between proportional and equal allocation
- Big challenge: selection of consistency evaluation criteria
- **Holistic approach** based on several candidate criteria recommended over fixed single criterion
- Summaries of operational characteristics such as probabilities of meeting candidate consistency criteria should be provided over a range of sample sizes

## Interpretations of Results

### Internal Consistency
- Consistency of treatment effects across multiple settings (regions, endpoints, subgroups)
- Evaluate: consistency of primary and secondary analyses, consistency across endpoints, consistency across subgroups
- Pooling strategy usually adopted to evaluate consistency across regions or subpopulations

### External Consistency
- Whether consistent treatment effect observed from multiple data sources
- Compare across Phase 3 registration trials and Phase 2 exploratory trials
- Examine whether treatment effects are consistent across trials and specific factors
- Integrated efficacy analysis can be used if needed

### Consistency of Benefit-Risk
- Comprehensive benefit-risk assessment for overall population
- Subsequently, consistency of benefit-risk between regions/overall population evaluated
- Includes: treatment background, clinical pharmacology, rationale, benefit, risk, benefit-risk

### Consistency of Safety
- Key elements: AEs, serious AEs, Grade ≥3 AEs, AEs leading to discontinuation
- Time to AE onset, duration, outcome, exposure-adjusted incidence
- Between-subject variability to support consistency evaluation

## Special Considerations

### Non-Inferiority
- Table 1: Non-inferiority margin-adjusted treatment effect
  - Absolute Metrics: $H_0: T - C = M$ vs $H_1: T - C > M (M \leq 0)$ → T-C+M
  - Relative Metrics: $H_0: T/C \leq M$ vs $H_1: T/C > M (M > 1)$ → T/(C*M)
- Treatment effect may be replaced with non-inferiority margin-adjusted treatment effect

### Multiple Primary Endpoints
- Usually two scenarios: (1) treatment effect on all primary endpoints, or (2) at least one
- Sample size adjusted by number of endpoints
- Multiplicity adjustment for multiple endpoints not required for consistency evaluation

### Adaptive Design
- Increasingly popular in MRCTs
- Adaptive sample size allocation may be considered
- Consistency should be maintained throughout interim analyses

### Single Arm Trial (SAT)
- Designed to be open-label without parallel control
- In some cases, SAT may be considered to support accelerated approval
- ORR point estimate of region/subpopulation evaluated
- Generally small sample size for overall population

### Rare Disease
- Limited data, limited R&D experience
- Multiple subtypes, high patient heterogeneity
- Limited sample size, narrow enrollment window

## Statistical Models for Analysis

### Fixed-Effect Model (FEM)
- Assumes same treatment effect across regions
- Continuous random-effect model (CREM) and discrete random-effect model (DREM) as alternatives

### Bayesian Hierarchical Models
- FDA and CDE perspective: three-level hierarchical structure
  - **Level 1**: Studies
  - **Level 2**: Subgroups (regions)
  - **Level 3**: Patients
- Can be utilized to estimate treatment effects for each region
- Reduce variability and improve robustness

## Exploratory Framework for Inconsistency
- When inconsistency with expectations is observed:
  1. **Clinical relevance**: Differences may be quantitatively large but not clinically relevant
  2. **Disease and treatment**: Epidemiology, diagnostics, treatments at time of data interpretation
  3. **Safety analysis**: Notable differences in safety measures between regional/overall population
  4. **Biological plausibility**: Degree of causality, pharmacological/clinical mechanistic considerations

## Key Equations
- **Non-inferiority absolute**: $H_0: \mu_T - \mu_C = M$ vs $H_1: \mu_T - \mu_C > M$, adjusted effect = $T - C + M$ ($M \leq 0$)
- **Non-inferiority relative**: $H_0: \mu_T / \mu_C \leq M$ vs $H_1: \mu_T / \mu_C > M$, adjusted effect = $T/(C \cdot M)$ ($M > 1$)

## Relevance to nABCD Paper
- **Complementary perspective**: Long et al. focus on *what* to evaluate for consistency; our nABCD paper provides *how* to quantify effect modifier similarity that underlies consistency
- **Gap identified**: The paper recommends holistic evaluation with "several candidate criteria" but lacks a quantitative metric for comparing effect modifier distributions — nABCD fills this gap
- **Pooling strategy justification**: nABCD provides pre-trial evidence for pooling decisions discussed in Section "Intrinsic and Extrinsic Factors, Pooling Strategy"
- **Bayesian hierarchical models**: Their proposed model structure could incorporate nABCD as an informative prior or covariate for regional borrowing
- **Regulatory context**: Authors from Peking University, CDE, NMPA — strengthens China/NMPA regulatory relevance alongside Song et al. (2025)
- **Simpson's Paradox**: Paper warns about avoiding Simpson's Paradox when interpreting pooling results — nABCD addresses this by ensuring distributional similarity before pooling
