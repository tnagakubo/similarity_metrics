# Singh et al. (2024) — Simultaneous Global Drug Development and MRCT: 5 Years After ICH E17

## Citation
Singh R, Wang W, Chakravarty A, Wang J, Uyama Y (2024) "Simultaneous Global Drug Development and Multiregional Clinical Trials (MRCT): 5 Years After Implementation of ICH E17 Guidelines" *Therapeutic Innovation & Regulatory Science* 58:845–854. DOI: [10.1007/s43441-024-00639-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s43441-024-00639-0)
- Received 2023-10-23 / Accepted 2024-03-01 / Published online 2024-05-12

## Author Authority (CRITICAL for positioning)
All authors **except Jun Wang were members of the ICH E17 Expert Working Group** that published the 2017 guideline. Affiliations:
- Rominder Singh (Pfizer R&D), William Wang (Merck), Aloka Chakravarty (former FDA Senior Statistical Advisor, Office of the Commissioner)
- **Jun Wang (NMPA / China CDE)**, **Yoshiaki Uyama (PMDA / Japan)** — i.e., the regulators behind both the China and Japan positions are co-authors.

→ This is a **first-party retrospective by the guideline's own authors**. Its admissions of unsolved problems carry maximal authority for a gap claim.

## Document Type (caution)
Labeled "ORIGINAL RESEARCH" but is in substance a **perspective / commentary**: no new data, no original analysis (only a PubMed hit-count observation). Strength of the paper as a paper is low; its value is **who said it**, not methodological rigor.

## Scope
Reflects on why uptake of some ICH E17 concepts (esp. **pooling strategy**) has been slow 5 years after implementation, and recommends starting an **ICH Q&A process** to modernize the guideline. Surveys paradigm shifts since 2017: personalized medicine, expedited pathways, patient-centricity/PROs, decentralized trials (DCT), RWE/RWD, limitations of ICH E5, the rise of small "Search & Develop" sponsors, COVID lessons, adaptive design, social determinants of health, digital pivot.

## Key Citable Passages (for our W₁ / pooling-distance paper)

### ① Pooling-strategy ambiguity is a *named main cause* of slow uptake (p.846)
> "In both the EU and Japan surveys, lack of clarity on defining the sample size and 'pooling strategies' was identified as one of the major reasons on lack of understanding of the guidelines."
- Primary sources: EFPIA survey [4] and JPMA survey [5]. Singh et al. report/endorse this.

### ② Pooling must be "scientifically sound" — but **no metric, basis, or bound is given** (p.848)
> "when pooling regions, it is crucial to ensure that the grouping is justified and scientifically sound. ... Factors such as the similarity of the patient populations, healthcare practices, standard of care, and regulatory requirements should be considered when deciding which regions to pool."
- This is exactly **our gap**: the EWG demands *similarity-based* justification but supplies no distance, threshold, or guarantee. Mirrors the 小宮山 TF report self-admission of "no clear basis for defining similarity" ([[project_e17_3layer_positioning]]).

### ③ "Lack of understanding and application of the 'pooled region'" (p.848)
> "various survey results have suggested that there is lack of understanding, and application, of the 'pooled region'."

### ④ Pooling is EM-based (vocabulary matches our Path α) (p.851)
> The pooling strategy "should be based on established knowledge about key intrinsic/extrinsic factor and effect modifiers (i.e., those intrinsic/extrinsic factors that impact the treatment effects)." Subjects "who have similar factors shall be defined prospectively" as pooled region or pooled subpopulation; consistency evaluation can take a **layered approach** (country / region / subpopulation / global).

### ⑤ Conclusion — pooling still needs clarification (p.853)
> "there are still topics that require clarification, such as pooling strategies, local vs. regional data for registration, and other operational challenges."

## How to Use in Our Paper
- **Introduction gap paragraph**: cite alongside EFPIA [4], JPMA [5], and 小宮山 TF report → three-layer evidence (guideline authors + industry + regulators) that a **principled pooling distance is missing**.
- **Attribution discipline (P5)**: ①/③ are survey-derived (attribute to EFPIA/JPMA); ②/④/⑤ are the authors' opinion — cite as "E17 working-group members note that…", not as data.
- Do **not** over-lean on this single source; it is a weak commentary. Bundle with primary surveys.

## Critical Weaknesses (Louis, reviewer view)
- Mislabeled "ORIGINAL RESEARCH" (no data/method).
- **Duplicate references**: [13] and [14] are the *same* NSCLC immunotherapy paper (Nat Rev Clin Oncol 20:423–4, 2023) with different author lists — copy-editing failure.
- "Slow uptake" chapter conflates *uptake problems* with *MRCT not being needed* (cell/gene therapy, precision oncology) — different issues.
- Recommendations lack specificity; conclusion is "start a Q&A" with no *how* — precisely the pooling-methodology void we fill.
- Typos (e.g., "Salim Y, Wittes J" = misattributed Yusuf S, Wittes J, NEJM 2016 [24]).
