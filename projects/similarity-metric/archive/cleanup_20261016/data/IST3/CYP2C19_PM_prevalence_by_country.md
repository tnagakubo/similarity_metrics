# CYP2C19 Poor Metabolizer (PM) Prevalence by Country/Population

## Context
- CYP2C19 loss-of-function (LoF) is a confirmed effect modifier for clopidogrel efficacy
- PM status = homozygous LoF (*2/*2, *2/*3, or *3/*3)
- Key evidence: Mega et al. (2009) NEJM — carriers of CYP2C19 reduced-function alleles had 53% increased risk of MACE with clopidogrel
- Relevant for nABCD case study: PM prevalence varies dramatically across countries (2-23%), creating a natural test case for distribution similarity

## Notes for nABCD Case Study

1. **Why CYP2C19 PM is ideal for nABCD demonstration:**
   - Confirmed effect modifier for clopidogrel (HR 1.53 for MACE in PMs vs non-carriers)
   - Dramatic variation across regions: 2% (Europe) to 18% (Japan) — roughly 9-fold
   - Well-characterized in large datasets with robust sample sizes
   - Clinically actionable (CPIC guidelines exist)
   - Binary classification (PM vs non-PM) simplifies distribution comparison

2. **Comparison with EGFR mutation case:**
   - EGFR: 2-67% variation (wider range)
   - CYP2C19 PM: 1.5-23% variation (narrower but still dramatic)
   - Both show clear East-West gradient
   - CYP2C19 has better country-level data from large-scale genotyping studies

3. **Data quality considerations:**
   - Ionova 2020 (23andMe) has the largest N but may have self-selection bias
   - Fricke-Galindo 2016 is the most comprehensive review of published literature
   - Country-specific estimates for East Asia are well-established
   - African and Latin American data remain sparse
