# Referee report — Quantifying Effect Modifier Similarity for Regional Pooling in Multi-Regional Clinical Trials
Target: Statistics in Medicine. Reviewed against `paper/per_em_W1_wiley.tex` (802 lines, commit 8308f84; PDF of 2026-10-17 14:30, 15 pp) and the recorded outputs in `results/` and `data/`.

## Summary (5 lines)
1. The paper proposes the per-effect-modifier Wasserstein-1 distance, calibrated through the Kantorovich–Rubinstein bound Δmax = L·W1, as a measure of how far apart two regions' effect-modifier distributions are. It evaluates the estimator in simulation and applies it to GUSTO-I.
2. Every application number and simulation-table cell I could recompute matches. That covers 30/30 W1 point estimates, all CIs to within Monte Carlo error, 6+1 unresolved distances, the Spearman coefficients, the scenario truths, the coverage counts and all 39 entries of the required-n table. The recomputation scripts are in this directory.
3. The mathematics is correct as stated (Proposition 1, the attaining CATE τ*, the monotone supremum, the KS comparison, the Jensen bias and the joint W_d bound). Two places need a condition: the null-floor "level 0.05" (approximate only) and sharpness on a bounded outcome scale.
4. The interpretation is weaker than the mathematics. The application never reports a single calibrated reading (§4.4). It reads two modifiers against one margin, which contradicts the single-modifier premise of Proposition 1. Its "wholly above" reading relies on CATEs that the paper's own Appendix A.2 shows to be infeasible on the mortality scale.
5. Source hygiene: two mid-line `%` comments change the compiled text. Line 785 hides a sentence; lines 620–621 produce "ranking.R2" and merge two paragraphs. The source to be submitted also carries several hundred lines of internal edit history and placeholders. Verdict: **major revision**.

## Overall assessment
The contribution, as delimited in §1 (l. 90) and §2.2 (l. 144), has three parts. The first is a formulation: a per-modifier distance in original units whose heterogeneity bound has a clinical slope as its constant. The second is an evaluation of whether the distance is blind or recovering on a selection task. The third is an evaluation of the estimator's bias and percentile-bootstrap coverage. All three are delivered, and the reporting is unusually candid: post hoc elements are disclosed (l. 461, 493, 551), unreported analyses are listed (l. 454), and the coverage count is stated against Monte Carlo error. Everything I could recompute matches. The aim, a measure that quantifies regional differences without prescribing a rule, is met at the level of the measure. It is not yet met at the level of the application. §4.4 states how an interval would be read against the reference distance but never reads one. The paper's main selling point, the clinical-scale translation (requirements ii and iii), therefore has no worked result anywhere in the manuscript.

What would make me reject in the present form is the clinical-calibration reading in §2.4/§4.4. First, it applies the per-modifier bound to age and SBP separately, each against the full Δclin = 1 point. Proposition 1 assumes that the CATE depends on that modifier alone, and it cannot depend on age alone and on SBP alone at the same time. Under the additive CATE that the Discussion itself (l. 725) names as the case in which per-modifier bounds are valid, the bounds add. R5 is "wholly below" on both modifiers, yet its two upper-limit contributions sum to 1.84 points. Second, the "wholly above" reading ("a slope within the bound could produce Δclin even at the lower confidence limit", l. 638) is true only over CATEs restricted by their slope alone. By the KS bound, any CATE that produces 1 point for R2, R3 or R16 on age must vary by at least 10.6–12.9 percentage points of absolute 30-day mortality across age. The overall t-PA-versus-SK effect in these data is 1.0 point and the mortality of patients aged ≤45 is 1.3%. Appendix A.2 already says that the attaining CATE lies "beyond any baseline risk", but neither the body nor the abstract nor the closing paragraph carries that qualifier.

### M3. "Sharp" and "could produce Δclin" are stated without the bounded-outcome qualifier that Appendix A.2 itself proves is binding
The supremum L·W1 over CATEs restricted only by their slope is attained by τ* (l. 792). That is correct (Mathematics check §2). But on a risk-difference scale τ = R1 − R0 is bounded, and Appendix A.2 (l. 789) says that the attaining τ* would need a range of 0.91 (age) or 0.56 (SBP), "beyond any baseline risk". The qualifier stays in the appendix. Four places state the property without it:
- §2.2 l. 144: "sharp when the slope is the only restriction".
- §2.4 l. 253: "some such CATE produces it whenever W1 ≥ Δclin/L_UB".
- §4.4 l. 638: "a slope within the bound could produce Δclin even at the lower confidence limit".
- §5 l. 735: "is sharp over that class".

The problem is quantitative, not cosmetic. The KS inequality |τ̄1 − τ̄2| ≤ TV(τ)·D_KS gives a necessary condition: any CATE that produces Δclin must have total variation at least Δclin/D_KS. For the three age partners read as "wholly above" (R2, R3, R16), D_KS = 0.078, 0.094 and 0.079. Any CATE producing a 1-point regional difference must therefore vary by at least 12.9, 10.6 and 12.6 percentage points of absolute 30-day mortality across age. For SBP (R9, R11, R12) the figures are 6.2–6.8 points. The data put these in context: the whole t-PA-versus-SK effect is 1.0 point (6.31% vs 7.32%), and mortality is 1.3% at age ≤ 45 (recompute_mortality.log). The "wholly above" reading therefore refers to CATEs the trial population cannot exhibit.

Defensible wording: "sharp over the class of CATEs restricted only by their slope; on a bounded effect scale the attaining CATE may be infeasible, and the bound is then conservative (Appendix A.2)". In §4.4, report Δclin/D_KS (or another feasibility check) next to the "wholly above" reading.

### M4. Two modifiers are each read against the whole margin, contrary to the premise of Proposition 1
Proposition 1 (l. 175) and the abstract (l. 44) require that the CATE "depends on the effect modifier X alone". The application treats age and SBP as candidate modifiers at once (l. 544) and reads each against Δclin = 1 point (l. 638, Figure 4). At most one of the two premises (age-only, SBP-only) can be true. If both modifiers act, the Discussion's own result (l. 725) applies: for an additive CATE the valid bound is the sum Σ_k L_k W1^(k), and under interaction even that sum can understate.

Consequence, from Table 5: R5 is "wholly below" on both modifiers (age upper limit 0.92 y → 0.92 points; SBP upper limit 4.59 mmHg → 0.92 points). Under an additive CATE within both bounds its regional difference can still reach 1.84 points, nearly twice the margin. So "an interval wholly below this distance means that … the distributional difference cannot produce such a regional difference" (l. 253; l. 638) is false as stated once more than one modifier is in play.

Fix: qualify each reading with "if the CATE depends on this modifier alone". Add the additive reading (sum of the per-modifier upper-limit contributions against Δclin), or state that each per-modifier reading bounds only that modifier's contribution.
