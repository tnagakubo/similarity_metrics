### 1.2 Is the sentence at l.73 faithful?
Sentence: "Song et al., writing on the NMPA's implementation of ICH E17, identify the pooling strategy as an area where ambiguity remains and describe clustering populations on distances defined from the correlation between effect modifiers and endpoints, citing the reference volume discussed next."

* "identify the pooling strategy as an area where ambiguity remains": supported, but the statement is in the **abstract (p.359)**, not p.362. The abstract says "several ambiguities" and lists pooling strategy among items that "remain a challenge".
* "describe clustering populations on distances defined from the correlation between effect modifiers and endpoints": supported by p.362. Missing qualifier: Song says this applies "when more than one EM is taken [into] consideration".
* "citing the reference volume": verified (ref [2]).
* **Verdict: faithful, not over-read.** It under-reports two things: the "more than one EM" condition, and that Song gives no formula, weighting, standardisation, or choice of distance.

### 1.3 Does Song et al. propose or evaluate a distance between regional EM distributions?
**No.** Song (i) states in one sentence that "the correlation between these EMs and endpoints can be used to define the distances between different populations" (the same idea as Komiyama 4.6.1.1, a weighting pointer, not a distance on EM distributions); (ii) specifies no distance function, scale, standardisation, or treatment of distributional shape; (iii) reports no simulation, data analysis or operating characteristics for the clustering. The only data-level statement is the PREVENT sentence ("countries with similar distributions of effect modifiers were grouped into five pooled regions"), which is taken from Matsushima 2024 and gives no measure of "similar".

What Song does NOT say (over-reading guard): that the correlation-based distance has been validated; any weighting scheme or Lasso; that clustering should decide pooling; any comparison of distributions; any data-driven number of clusters (only "generally no more than four", citing Alosh 2017 and Quan 2010).

### 1.4 Does the novelty claim survive?
Abstract: "to our knowledge, no distance between regional effect modifier distributions has been evaluated for this purpose"; l.75: "to our knowledge no distance between regional effect modifier distributions has been proposed and evaluated for this purpose". **Both survive Song 2025**, and also survive Komiyama Ch.4 (Euclidean / Manhattan / correlation distances on "a representative value in each candidate parameter", no evaluation) and Singh et al. 2024 (`knowledge/pdfs/e639.txt` = *Ther Innov Regul Sci* 58:845-854, DOI 10.1007/s43441-024-00639-0; pooling at principle level only; no cluster or distance passage).
It remains a "to our knowledge" claim. Falsifier: a paper that defines and evaluates a distance on the full EM distributions across regions. **Open item**: how Matsushima 2024 measured "a similar distribution of effect modifiers" in PREVENT is in their Data S1 / Table S1, which is not in the knowledge base (Paper Request #2). Until read, the status is "not contradicted by anything on disk", not "verified absent". Do not strengthen to "no one has defined".

### 1.5 Proposed wording (one sentence, replaces the Song sentence at l.73)

> Song et al., writing on the NMPA's implementation of ICH E17, list the pooling strategy among the areas where ambiguity remains and state that, when more than one effect modifier is considered, the correlation between the effect modifiers and the endpoints can be used to define distances between populations for hierarchical or k-means clustering, citing the reference volume discussed next without specifying the distance or evaluating it.\cite{song2025}

Shorter variant (current sentence plus the missing condition): "... and describe, when more than one effect modifier is considered, clustering populations on distances defined from the correlation between effect modifiers and endpoints, citing the reference volume discussed next."

### 1.6 Related finding next to Minor 11: `matsushima2024` at l.75 is NOT SUPPORTED
Manuscript l.75: "A workshop report by Matsushima et al. documented that regional imbalance in CRP-positive/MRI-negative status contributed to apparent inconsistency in the secukinumab MRCT. This example illustrates the practical consequences when effect modifier distributional differences are not assessed quantitatively at the planning stage."

Original (Matsushima 2024, *Clin Pharmacol Ther* 115(5):965-970, DOI [10.1002/cpt.3163](https://doi.org/10.1002/cpt.3163), Case 1, p.966): "Post hoc analyses of pooling regions or subpopulations were exploratory attempted. Due to the small number of patients in each participating country, countries with a similar distribution of effect modifiers were clustered into five pooled regions (Data S1, Table S1). ... The characteristics of the participants in region 1, including Japan, showed a lower proportion of CRP+ patients and a higher proportion of MRI+ patients. The treatment difference in region 1 was smaller than that in the overall population. ... The treatment differences were generally consistent among the pooled regions for commonalities in the effect modifiers, but differed slightly depending on the distribution of CRP+/MRI+ and sex (Figure S2). **The inconsistent results observed in the Japanese subpopulation could be due to an imbalance in treatment assignment and a small sample size (2 participants in the secukinumab arm and 7 in the placebo arm), but not due to potential effect modifiers.**" Table 2 ("Statistical uncertainty") repeats: "The results in the Japanese population are considered by chance due to the imbalanced treatment assignment and small sample size rather than an effect of CRP/MRI abnormalities and sex."

Problems: (a) the source attributes the Japanese inconsistency to treatment-assignment imbalance and small n, **not** to effect modifiers, the opposite of the manuscript sentence; (b) the regional pattern was fewer CRP+ and more MRI+ (not "CRP-positive/MRI-negative"; CRP+/MRI- appears in the source only as a single-positive subgroup label); (c) the pooling was post hoc and exploratory, so "not assessed quantitatively at the planning stage" is our inference, not the source's. Suggested replacement:

> In a post hoc pooling of the PREVENT trial in non-radiographic axial spondyloarthritis, a workshop report by Matsushima et al. clustered countries with a similar distribution of effect modifiers into five pooled regions and found treatment differences that varied slightly with the distribution of CRP and MRI positivity and sex, while attributing the inconsistent Japanese result to imbalanced treatment assignment and a small sample size rather than to effect modifiers.\cite{matsushima2024}

A following sentence such as "The pooled regions there were formed from effect modifier distributions, which is the step a distance between regional distributions would quantify" is our reading and may be kept or dropped. Related P5 issue: re-check the CLAIMS row that carries the secukinumab example.
